Analysis

AKA "What is my center?"

Every new iteration of my portfolio starts with a core, a center. Something to sprout ideas off of and grow, but always coming back to the core. For applications, I tend to call this ‘one lining it' as to one line of what the application does, but portfolios can be more expressive art concepts, so abstract works here.

Today's choice of center… flow. It needs to be grounded in the "what's next", "how did I get here?" Being able to display connections and links is important and the 1 - 2 - 3 pathway for reasoning and intent.

Wireframes

AKA "How about let's not do that..."

For the Home screen:

I start with a box for structure that’s about the size of a larger screen. Inside the box, I lay out the header, which for now is the name of the site and a link to LinkedIn.

After that, what matters is the individual projects. How are they accessed? A card works well enough here, so it’s a good starting point. I lay out the thumbnail, project name and a quick ipsum blurb to place hold for a summary. I place all these elements in a box and start moving it around. I make it a master element in Axure, create a copy, then move the two around as a group.

I add the tag line, then start moving the two grouped cards and it around. Deciding I want the tag to take more of a priority, I leave it on the left, then stack the cards on the right to better utilize space.

Once I’m satisfied with the larger screen version, I make a copy of the page. On that page, I resize the background structure box to a smaller screen’s width, and anything outside of the box, I move, preferring what was once a left to right, top to bottom flow, now just flows top to bottom. I do resize the header’s text as it doesn’t need to be so large on smaller screens.

For the Project screen:

First is the navigation, I go back to the idea of flow and use a flowing step navigation consisting of arrows. Each step points to the next.

For the most part, I leave the content area alone, only adding emphasis to the sub-heading.

Displaying images are the biggest piece here. They need to be visible and easy to navigate. I use a standard gallery: main image with a thumbnail set. I consider a carousel, but there’s a time and a place for them. They also don’t give the control over the image selection I want.

For smaller screens, I complete the same process I did for the Home screen. I copy the Project page and adjust the flow. Although there are a couple of issues: the secondary navigation and the gallery.

The secondary navigation is easy enough to shrink into a menu. That’s pretty standard behavior.

The gallery is harder. I start with variations for this. The first one I try displays all the images to the side. It’s a decent start and allows the images to still be seen. When an image is clicked, the idea is it’ll open to display an image. However, this does not give maximum width for images, especially ones that will be wider than tall. The next attempt, I hide the images all together and have a toggle bar to open and close the gallery, replacing the text. The layout is the same on the larger screens, just on a smaller scale.

Mockups

AKA "Let's not call it jazz hands, shall we?"

Mockups are a place to add color and style. Today's color choice says ‘zen garden'. Colors should have a calm and serving purpose, creating pathways to lead and guide. Sparks of color should create attention, dragging visitors towards places of contemplation. A good cornerstone is blue for the professionalism it brings, with side swipes of purple and green. Color theory is fun, isn't it?

Color palette on Palleton

I copy the Axure file I was using for the wireframes to create a baseline file for the mockups, removing all the variations of pages I won't be using.

With blue being the primary color, I make a pass over the navigation and make it blue (Axure's widget system is helpful for this). The darker shades for active items or items I want to bring attention to. The lighter blue for items that can still be clicked.

I use the purples to add accents to non-clickable items: headings and decoration.

Reflection

AKA "Keep moving forward."

Once the site is complete, it's about adding more content and refining it. More projects.

It's also about adjusting and polishing. As I find something isn't working, I'll fix it. What you're browsing now isn't going to be what's documented here. I learn and know more. I get better. I change things. It's a never ending cycle.

Granted, the vice of most designers is knowing when to close the code files... Or the Photoshop files... or the InDesign files. At some point, you find a place where it's "good enough".

At that point, it's about knowing it's okay to put it away and just...

move forward.
