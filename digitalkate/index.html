Analysis

Every new iteration of my portfolio starts with a core, a center. Something to sprout ideas off of and grow, but always coming back to the core. For applications, I tend to call this ‘one lining it’ as to one line of what the application does, but portfolios can be more expressive art concepts, so abstract works here.

Today’s choice of center… flow. It needs to be grounded in the “what’s next”, “how did I get here?” Being able to display connections and links is important and the 1 - 2 - 3 pathway for reasoning and intent.

Wireframes

For the Home screen:

The first thing I do is add the elements I need on the screen: a heading, a connection to LinkedIn, something about myself, and a way to access the content within the site. These are core.

From there, it’s deciding the order. What’s important here? What should be seen first?

For the Project screen:

I start with the core elements of the page: a heading, text area, images. And again, back to order, what’s important, what’s priority?

Then it’s navigation. I go back to the idea of flow and use a flowing step navigation consisting of arrows. Each step points to the next.

The first mockup stage, I take the Axure files I was using and add color.

The baseline color for this project will be blue. It’s a standard professional color. It’s calming and can be bright without being wild. The items that need to stand out here are the images, not the color of the site.

I add just enough color to the site to accentuate items. Everything doesn’t need to be colored. Not everything needs attention.

After completing the first round, I noticed there’s something missing. The home page is a bit dull.

I send out the site to friends and colleagues for feedback. It’s the same consensus. Too much white space.

I grab the initial home page design and start varying it by adding more decoration. Options for this include more images and a background. For the background options, I lighten it considerably so it adds filling but isn’t distracting.

I also use this round to update the heading bar. Removing unneeded items to focus on the projects.

The last revision is to the gallery. After having built the site, the flow of the images and thumbnails isn’t working out.

I start on some new layouts with different content to gallery relationships. In the end, I settle on moving the gallery out of the page and into its own viewer. It allows for more space for the wireframe images.

I add a button and teaser images to the content area to toggle to the gallery to give quick access to it.

Reflection

Once the site is complete, it’s about adding more content and refining it. More projects.

It’s also about adjusting and polishing. As I find something isn’t working, I’ll fix it. What you’re browsing now isn’t going to be what’s documented here. I learn and know more. I get better. I change things. It’s a never ending cycle.

Granted, the vice of most designers is knowing when to close the code files... Or the Photoshop files… or the InDesign files. At some point, you find a place where it’s ‘good enough’.

At that point, it’s about knowing it’s okay to put it away and just…

move forward.
